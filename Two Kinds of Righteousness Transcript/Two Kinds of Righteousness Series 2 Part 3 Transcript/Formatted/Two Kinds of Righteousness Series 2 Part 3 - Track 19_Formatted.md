It is part of human history. From Genesis 1, 2, 3 and 4 it is clear that war was not in the seed of the earth. War is not in the plan of God. But God found himself in man's wars. Not that war was his plan. Let us see a few things again in that progressive nature of God's dealings in the Old Testament.

Exodus 23:27–30
[27] I will send my fear before thee, and will destroy all the people to whom thou shalt come, and I will make all thine enemies turn their backs unto thee.
[28] And I will send hornets before thee, which shall drive out the Hivite, the Canaanite, and the Hittite, from before thee.
[29] I will not drive them out from before thee in one year; lest the land become desolate, and the beast of the field multiply against thee.
[30] By little and little I will drive them out from before thee, until thou be increased, and inherit the land.

Notice the word "destroy" there does not mean to kill. It simply means that they will not come and hurt you. "I will send fear" — how? Verse 28 — "I will send hornets before thee; they shall drive out the Canaanites, the Hittites from before thee." "I will not drive them out from before thee in one year, lest the land become desolate and the beast of the field become multiplied against thee." So everything there is preventive. "I will ensure it does not happen at once, so that animals do not multiply and come and destroy."

*There was a man I read of this morning, just about a few hours ago, who was keeping lions — I think, or tigers — from a cub. They grew up together and they lived together for years. Just last week, one of them got angry and simply ate him up. Not serious. So that is what God is preventing here — he said it would be gradual.*

Now look at the word "hornets." Hornets — from the Hebrew word *ṣir'āh* — T-S-I-R-A-H — from another word *ṣāra'ath* — T-S-A-R-A-T-H — which is a metaphor for fear and dread. Some have said it is like the use of bees, the sound of bees, to scare people so they will run far.

Deuteronomy 1:44
[44] And the Amorites, which dwelt in that mountain, came out against you, and chased you, as bees do, and destroyed you in Seir, even unto Hormah.

Interestingly, some translations have this word as leprosy. But it cannot be so, because it says they will run. It is something that makes them run away. So it is to drive away an impending army. The word "hornets," therefore, is to drive away an impending army.

Now, I would love to use correlations. For example, in Exodus 14, the issue of the Red Sea — "Hold your peace; the Lord will fight for you. Fear not and stand still."

Exodus 14:13–14
[13] And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will shew to you to day: for the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever.
[14] The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.

God parted the sea. And then what he simply did for the Egyptians — it was not to rescue them. No. They saw the Egyptians see his miracles and his signs and wonders by parting the sea. And obviously it took a lot of hardened heart to see that and then go into that sea and perish. So what God does here is to prevent harm by not saving those who did not want him to save them — just like in God's new heaven and new earth, the person who does not believe the Gospel will not be saved. And God therefore prevents evil and good from dwelling together, which is justice and righteousness. We explored that before.

2 Kings 19:32–35

His own sons smote him. Now look at the correlation of this story in 2 Chronicles 32:20–23:

2 Chronicles 32:20–23

So what happened here was self-destruction. Isaiah 37:33–36 records it again — "They will not come near you; I will defend this city." What simply happened was that they faced themselves and fought themselves. So you see all these preventive things. Of course that element of the angel of the Lord also comes to mind. As we have explored so far, the angel of the Lord — or the angels of God — at that point will only carry out activities based on man's understanding and what was present in their world. That is why their ministries will take a new turn when it comes to Jesus.

Let us look at New Testament examples. We see Elymas the sorcerer who was simply prevented from further confusing the deputy — Acts 13 — or Paul himself, who was prevented from going further to carry out the execution of the saints. So what is going on here in the use of hornets is that all God wanted to use against the enemies of Israel was panic and discomfort. Today you can say rubber bullets, sprays, water spray.

The word "bees" in Deuteronomy 1:44 — the word is *debōrāh* — D-O-V-A-H — some have it as Deborah. You see it in Judges 14:8, Psalm 118:12, Isaiah 7:18. It is a sound, a huge sound. Isaiah 7:18 is a sound, a loud sound. From the word *dbb*, it is therefore a metaphor when the use of hornets is used — a sound panic.

So you see that God is simply walking through a nation that had been used to war, a warlike time in their world.

Exodus 13:17–18
[17] And it came to pass, when Pharaoh had let the people go, that God led them not through the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near; for God said, Lest peradventure the people repent when they see war, and they return to Egypt.
[18] But God led the people about, through the way of the wilderness of the Red sea: and the children of Israel went up harnessed out of the land of Egypt.

Exodus 14:13–14
[13] And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD…
[14] The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.

Why would he say that? Not because he is given to fight. He will get involved in it to prevent it — to prevent them from being harmed. And these words are recorded in what we call the New Testament as done in righteousness.

Acts 7:45
[45] Which also our fathers that came after brought in with Jesus into the possession of the Gentiles, whom God drave out before the face of our fathers, unto the days of David.

This is the Jericho-Joshua scenario.

Hebrews 11:30–34
[30] By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, after they were compassed about seven days.
[32] And what shall I more say? for the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae; of David also, and Samuel, and of the prophets:
[33] Who through faith subdued kingdoms, wrought righteousness, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions,
[34] Quenched the violence of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight, turned to flight the armies of the aliens.

Look at the word "turned to flight" — made them run away. In verse 33, "they wrought righteousness, obtained promises." Just like the Phinehas example. Is it God's will? No. God's method is by Spirit in Jesus. But that is God's permission — what God got involved in, that he never initiated. He just mitigates the violence and danger involved.

So when you read Hebrews 11, the first thing that comes to your mind is God's tolerance, God's accommodation — in Abel, in Noah, in Enoch, in Abraham, in Isaac, in Moses. But the finisher of it — Hebrews 12:2 — is Jesus. So I look at Jesus, then go back and reread the Old Testament and see that everything was progressing toward God's perfect day. The perfect day of Genesis 2 is the day of Jesus Christ. So I cannot read the progress — the journey — as the destination.

The journey is that God had to be part of the war — he did not initiate it. The journey involves the temple; the journey involves the use of swords; the journey involves this and that — all found in man's weaknesses. But it ends up in the death of Jesus. A contrast, a paradox, an irony — which means that all along, violence was from man and found in man's activities. And just as God accommodated it in the Old Testament, Jesus bore it in his death. Therefore the epistles will use the same imageries of war in Christian ministry and the Christian walk.

Which means that what the law could not do — in that it was weak through the flesh — through wars, through capital punishment — God was not absent in the law. God walked through the weakness of it until his Son. The law was a schoolmaster until Christ.

Exodus 12:12
[12] For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD.

What did God do to execute judgment? He preserved Israel and he made themselves destruct. We need to realize that sin is spiritual and it is self-destructive. Jesus exposed it through the authorities, the government, the religious bodies that got a hold of him and killed him.

When you get to Revelation, you still find war. But who is leading this war? A Lamb. How did Jesus enter Jerusalem? As a king — on a donkey. He did not enter with soldiers. How did he inaugurate his kingdom? He died. Why that paradox? It is so that all along death reigned from Adam through Moses.

Romans 5:14
[14] Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam's transgression…

God was right there in his permissiveness. "In the beginning was light in darkness." "The day you eat of it, you will die." "The seed of the woman will bruise the head of the serpent. I will put enmity between your seed and her seed." And that enmity continued. God walked through it until Jesus.

## Revelation and the Epistles: The Old Testament Imagery Fulfilled in the Spirit

So we therefore will see the New Testament gradually being revealed in the Old Testament as a book. Revelation now tells you — how did we overcome? "By the blood of the Lamb and the word of our testimony, not loving our lives even unto death."

Revelation 12:11
[11] And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

How can you overcome in death? Succumbing to evil is in itself victory over evil. "By the blood of the Lamb" means the sacrifice of Jesus. Again, a paradox of a sort and narrowly so. We say war was part of human life. War was in the fallen nature of man.

So if God wants to war against unrighteousness in their world, they will bring out a sword. He will bring out his Spirit. But what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sent his own Son. And in the likeness of sinful flesh he will condemn sin in the flesh — that the righteousness of the law, that the goal of it, is now fulfilled in us who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

2 Corinthians 10:3–5
[3] For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh:

Again, where is the narrative from? The narrative is from the Old Testament, where Israel's wars were largely theological, fought in the flesh. But we do not fight in the flesh. The weapons of our warfare — the word there is *strateuō* — S-T-R-A-T-E-U-O — the word in Luke 3:14 and 1 Corinthians 9:7, used literally for warfare. But Paul will say, what is this warfare?

It is fought by prophecy and by words.

James 4:1

A war not against the Amalekites now, not the Philistines — it is now the works of the flesh.

1 Peter 2:11
[11] Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul.

Of course, the Amalekites and the Philistines were idol worshipers who were the symbols of the works of the flesh. The preaching of the Gospel is fighting wars. Going to a territory to claim it for God is like the walls of the Old Testament. But what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son fulfilled it in us.

So which means that if I am astute enough in this — and we are all growing up, getting there too — I should be able to use the stories of the Old Testament properly, rightly dividing the word of truth, to yet teach war today. But you must have laid the foundation very well: this is God accommodating it. This is God not being the initiator. They would have done this anyway. Light over darkness. The weapons of our warfare — Paul uses the word *hoplon* — H-O-P-L-O-N. Go to John 18, you will see it literally used for soldiers. ## The Language of the Cross Embedded in Pauline Warfare

2 Corinthians 10:4–5
[4] (For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds;)
[5] Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ.

Pulling down strongholds — the word there is *kathairesis* — K-A-T-H-A-I-R-E-S-I-S — to pull down. And Paul will say, "We are pulling down strongholds." Yet in the same 2 Corinthians 10:8 he says, "I did not say all these things to pull you down." In verse 4, "We pull down strongholds." 2 Corinthians 10:8 — "Not for your destruction, not for your pulling down." 2 Corinthians 13:10 — "Not for your pulling down."

So what are we pulling down? Strongholds. What else are we casting down? *kathairō* — K-A-T-H-A-I-R-O — we are pulling down imaginations, to take down. I see Paul going to the cross here again.

Mark 15:36
[36] And one ran and filled a spunge full of vinegar, and put it on a reed, and gave him to drink, saying, Let alone; let us see whether Elias will come to take him down.

[46] And he bought fine linen, and took him down, and wrapped him in the linen, and laid him in a sepulchre which was hewn out of a rock, and rolled a stone unto the door of the sepulchre.

Mark is playing on words here. "They took him down." And Paul says, "We will now take down every imagination."

Luke 1:52
[52] He hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree.

The prophecy of the Messiah — he will put down the mighty from their seats.

[53] And he took it down, and wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a sepulchre that was hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid.

Acts 13:19
[19] And when he had destroyed seven nations in the land of Chanaan, he divided their land to them by lot.

When he had "destroyed" the seven nations — go back to read it again; it means he drove them out.

Acts 13:29

They took Jesus down from the tree. Acts 19:27 — the same word. So when Paul says "casting down imaginations," he is using a cross language. "And every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God" — that is theological. What Israel could not do in the flesh, God has sent us to do by his Spirit. As we go around the nations of the earth preaching the gospel, we have the whole armor of God.

The last statement he says here: "bringing into captivity" — *aichmalōtizō* — A-I-C-H-M-A-L-O-T-I-Z-O — to bring into captivity.

Those imaginations that exalt themselves against the knowledge of God were all captured. And when he was taken down to the sepulchre, they were as it were cast down. "We are casting down the imaginations." The word "imagination" is the word *logismos* — a thought pattern — and *noēma* — N-O-E-M-A — words he had used earlier.

2 Corinthians 2:11
[11] Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.

The thoughts of the deceiver.

[3] But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.

Their minds corrupted.

2 Corinthians 3:14

And of course Philippians 4:7, talking about the mind of Christ.

Just like Luke 11:22, against the "wiles of the devil" — *methodeia* — M-E-T-H-O-D-E-I-A — the tricks of the devil, just like Ephesians 4:14.

So they fought in the flesh what we fight by the Spirit. Go read Isaiah 42; 50; 54 — you see the armor of righteousness, the armor of God setting things right as they ought to be.

## The Progressive Unveiling of Satan in Redemptive History

Let me quickly just add this, and we will close. Today, for example, we have seen the huge gap between how Satan appears in the Old Testament and then suddenly in the four Gospels, where he is clear and identified. There has to be history to it.

We said that within the period after the last books of the prophets were written and before the four Gospels — the Matthew era — we had what they usually refer to as the Second Temple period. In that period, teachers took hold of the 39 books and began to explain them in Greek — for example, the Septuagint. It was in that era that the concept of *diabolos* — devil — became clear. They looked for a word in their day: the *diabolos*, a deceiver, a diabolical trickster, which is used even for people. *I am told that sometimes it is used in theaters.* And then they said, "There is a *diabolos*." So by the time Jesus came, that was the thought in the minds of people already — that all the evil being done in the Old Testament had now been explained in a language understood by them as the work of a *diabolos*.

So Jesus therefore used the words of the Second Temple era, which is after Nehemiah. The Second Temple era — find Nehemiah, also Ezra, Zechariah, Jeremiah, Daniel. They were all prophets speaking about the same things. And therefore came the concept of Satan, or ha-satan. The word *satan* is used for an opponent. That is why when you trace the scripture properly, you discover that it was used for God himself.

Numbers 22:22
[22] And God's anger was kindled because he went: and the angel of the LORD stood in the way for an adversary against him…

I will leave it here and expand it more in our next class. So you see that something happened that made the language what it is. Just like the writing of God was progressively revealed, Satan was also progressively unveiled. No doubt. But there is also a language to it — that is the causative and permissive use of the Hebrew language. And then the different dictionary grammar of the Greek vis-à-vis the Aramaic also made that difference. And just like God's will was progressively known, Satan was also progressively revealed. Just like God's word is known by language, Satan is also known by language.

As I said, in Numbers 22, we will see next week that the word *satan* was equally used for God. Just like when you say "Lucifer, light bearer" — and then you come to the epistles and find that the word "day star" was used both for the devil and for us, who shine as lights in the world. We will pick that up from there.
