## 1. Introduction: The Nature And Origin Of Workers In Church History

A workers' convention like this, or Workers in Training Convention as we call it in Saints Community Church, is a special meeting.

The individuals here are selected, not indiscriminately, but they are people who have shown willingness to serve and willingness to be available to serve other saints.

So we call them workers.

Naturally, you won't see that phrase in scripture.

I guess one of the things we have to include in our book, Signs of the Times, is workers and non-workers, almost looking like employed and unemployed.

You won't find that phrase in scripture.

Over time, we have seen that based on historical developments, some things are historical developments.

That is, you won't necessarily see them in scripture, but historically they have come up; that is, they have been developed.

Sometimes it has become a doctrine, and we must be careful not to make it such. That is why we are doing our best not to adopt it.

You will notice in Matthew 9:36, Matthew records where Jesus had gone round the cities. He repeats Matthew 4:23 in Matthew 9:35; it is like word-for-word verbiage.

Then he says Jesus looked on the multitude, that is, having crowds of people.

They were scattered abroad, and he had compassion on them.

Why did he have compassion on them?

Don't forget he had taught them the word. He had preached the gospel of the kingdom to them. He had healed all manner of sickness and diseases. He had also cleansed the lepers.

Yet he said he had compassion on them because they were like sheep that had no shepherd.

That is interesting, because one would have thought a church service is so glorious when people hear the word, when their sicknesses are cured and healed, and when they receive miracles.

That should pass for a glorious convention. By many parameters today, that works; that is a good meeting.

Yet he still had compassion.

At the end of a convention, after people have said, 'See what the Lord has done; he healed my body, he cast out demons from my body,' and everyone felt that was good, he still had compassion on them, for they were like sheep having no shepherd.

## 4. The Great Commission: Making Disciples Of All Nations

We are going to look at what to do with a new convert or a new member: what do you do? You make disciples.

In Matthew 28:18, Jesus said, 'All authority is given unto me in heaven and on earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations.'

The word teach there means to make disciples. Matheteuo: to make disciples of all nations.

Baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.

Why does the King James Version use teach? Because you cannot have disciples without teaching.

Verse 20 shows you the emphasis: 'Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you.'

Look at the keyword: 'whatsoever,' or 'all things whatsoever.'

If you put that together with 2 Timothy 2:2, 'the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men.'

He says the things, all things whatsoever.

Those two texts mean that you are not going to select what to pass across and what not to pass across.

All things whatsoever you heard of me.

You are not meant to invent it. You are not meant to sit down and think, 'What will work?' No.

You are not meant to say, 'What will people like?' No.

He said, 'Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.'

When we say, 'What to do to a new convert, what to do to a new member,' we must look at what Jesus did, and not just Jesus, but what the early apostles of Christ did, who laid for us the foundation.

## 5. The Calling Of The First Disciples: Following Is Deliberate And Sensitive

When he says to make disciples, how did Jesus make disciples?

What do you do to a new member or a new convert? You make disciples.

That short word, which requires explanation, must always be in your mind: to make disciples.

People can drop that word very easily and say, 'He's my disciple, she's my disciple,' and they may not even know what they are talking about.

Have it in your mind: to make disciples. What do you mean by making disciples? Notice that word: to make.


**Matthew 4:17–22 (KJV)**

**[17] From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.**

**[18] And Jesus, walking by the sea of Galilee, saw two brethren, Simon called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea: for they were fishers.**

**[19] And he saith unto them, Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.**

**[20] And they straightway left their nets, and followed him.**

**[21] And going on from thence, he saw other two brethren, James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, in a ship with Zebedee their father, mending their nets; and he called them.**

**[22] And they immediately left the ship and their father, and followed him.**

Don't miss the crucial details: they left and followed him.

This is a very sensitive work, because he has asked them to follow him.

When you ask people to follow you, you must be worthy of following.

You must know the seriousness of what it means for another human being to follow you.

You must know the sensitive nature, because exactly what he told them to do in Matthew 28:19–20 is what he did with them: 'Follow me, and I will make you.'

'I will make you.' That is what he said to Peter: 'I will make you.'

It is a sensitive work; you must ensure that you are worth following.


**Mark 1:16–20 (KJV)**

**[16] Now as he walked by the sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Andrew his brother casting a net into the sea: for they were fishers.**

**[17] And Jesus said unto them, Come ye after me, and I will make you to become fishers of men.**

**[18] And straightway they forsook their nets, and followed him.**

**[19] And when he had gone a little further thence, he saw James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, who also were in the ship mending their nets.**

**[20] And straightway he called them: and they left their father Zebedee in the ship with the hired servants, and went after him.**


**Luke 5:10–11 (KJV)**

**[10] And so was also James, and John, the sons of Zebedee, which were partners with Simon. And Jesus said unto Simon, Fear not; from henceforth thou shalt catch men.**

**[11] And when they had brought their ships to land, they forsook all, and followed him.**

We have three accounts that show us how they became his disciples.

They had to make decisions based on his direction.

It therefore means that the one calling people to follow him or her must be worth following.

If you are that person, it therefore means you need to realize the sensitive nature of what you are doing.

It means therefore that your conduct matters. It matters a lot: the way you live your life.

Your decisions also matter: the kind of decisions you take. You cannot say, 'Well, it's my life, I can live it the way I want.' No, you can't say that, because you just told someone to follow you.

If you are going to tell someone to follow you, then they must be in your consideration.

They must be in your decision making.

It is a sensitive work. You don't use 'He is my disciple' in a loose way.

Your conduct, your decisions, your actions, and how you correct yourself in your inactions or misactions is vital.

It is not possible for someone to just walk up to another person by the riverside and say, 'Hello, good morning, follow me,' and they drop their net and follow.

You would call the police. That would be charm, or hypnotism, or something criminal.

It is not possible for someone to just come and say, 'You are fishermen, don't worry, you no longer fish fish, you fish men, let's go.' No.

The reason why he was able to say that to them was because they had known him.

They had known him before then; they weren't seeing him for the first time.

How did they know Jesus? Look at Acts of the Apostles chapter 1.

## 8. The Definition Of A Disciple And Concluding Charge

Who is a disciple?

A disciple is a disciplined follower of a focused leader.

A disciplined follower of a focused leader.

Remember we started with sheep having no shepherd, and that sheep having no shepherd has a direction.

A disciplined follower of a focused leader.

This leader must be focused, number one, in his lifestyle.

In 1 Corinthians 4:17, Paul says, 'I have sent unto you Timotheus, who is my beloved son, and faithful in the Lord, who shall bring you into remembrance of my ways which be in Christ, as I teach every where in every church.'

It is not just doctrine; Timothy knows Paul's lifestyle.

Focused leadership will be seen in the conduct, number one.

Number two, in the message.

And number three, in your relationships.

When you choose to make disciples, it is a lifetime job.

You want to lead the person all life long. A lifetime job.

It doesn't mean the person will always be in your church. Some will be angry and walk away; some will even fight you.

Make sure you are still leading, and you are leading aright.

Who is a disciple? A disciplined follower of a focused leader.

Jesus taught everybody deep insights and revelations. In Matthew 7:28–29, they were astonished at his doctrine, for he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.

That is not how it works for disciples. To disciples, he said, 'Follow me.'

Disciples don't just get information; they obey instructions.

That somebody sits in your meetings is not enough. That somebody hears you preach is not enough.

It is the people who follow your instructions.

After they had been to Jesus's meetings and liked his teaching, he said, 'Follow me,' and they dropped all they had and followed him.

If you are going to do that to people, then you must take responsibility.

You can't guess. You can't experiment. You must be focused.
